Case 2 · Reports 04 Quality and 05 Biosafety

Impossible Foods
leghemoglobin.

Leghemoglobin is the soybean protein that makes the Impossible Burger look and taste like meat. The company makes it in engineered yeast, Pichia pastoris. Its first FDA filing stalled. The second one is the clearest public example of a specification and safety dossier for a protein made by a microbe.

The dossier in four numbers

FDA accepted the second filing, GRN 737, in July 2018. These four numbers carry most of its argument.

≥65%

Minimum share of total protein that must be leghemoglobin. Five lots measured 71–86%.

Why the first filing stalled

The protein is made inside the yeast. To get it out, the cells are broken open, so everything else the yeast makes comes along. The first filing described leghemoglobin well and said little about the rest.

  1. Sep 2014 · GRN 540 filed. Leghemoglobin as a flavour, up to 1.4% of the product.
  2. 2015 · FDA reviewers question the safety argument. Press reports later describe more than 40 other yeast proteins making up as much as a quarter of the ingredient, none of them characterised.
  3. Nov 2015 · Impossible Foods withdraws the filing.
  4. Oct 2017 · GRN 737 filed with a purity floor, an analysis of the 17 most abundant yeast proteins, and rat feeding studies. Six amendments follow.
  5. Jul 2018 · FDA replies with "no questions", the outcome a GRAS notice aims for.
  6. Aug 2019 · Separate approval as a colour additive, because the red colour of raw meat analogue counts as colouring. FDA adds the ≥65% purity minimum to the law itself (21 CFR 73.520).
  7. 2019–21 · Center for Food Safety objects and sues. FDA denies the objections; the Ninth Circuit upholds the approval in May 2021.

The 2015 FDA memos are known only through press reports of documents released under freedom-of-information requests. Every other date is from FDA and court documents.

How to write it

WeakThe leghemoglobin preparation is highly pure and free of contaminants.

"Highly pure" has no limit and no method. "Free of contaminants" is untestable, because nobody can test for every contaminant.

StrongLeghemoglobin made up 71–86% of total protein across five independent lots (specification ≥65%, SDS-PAGE with densitometry). The remaining protein is from the yeast host; its 17 most abundant proteins were identified and screened against allergen databases.

Range, n, limit, method, and a plain statement of what the rest of the material is.

Report 05: safety and containment

The safety argument is one chain of arithmetic. Anyone can redo it from the notice, which is why it persuades.

1 · Use

25 g of burger a day × 0.8% leghemoglobin = 200 mg/day.

2 · Heavy eaters

90th percentile taken as twice the mean: 400 mg/day ÷ 60 kg = 6.67 mg/kg/day.

3 · Rat study

28-day feeding, no adverse effect at the top dose: 750 mg/kg/day leghemoglobin.

4 · Compare

750 ÷ 100 safety factor = 7.5 mg/kg/day allowed, above the 6.67 eaten.

From GRN 737 Tables 2–3 and Fraser et al. 2018. A later 90-day study (Reyes et al. 2023) found no adverse effect at the highest dose either. The 2019 colour additive petition used newer intake data with higher numbers; do not mix the two sets.

What the dossier says about the organism

Strain

Host strain from a known safe lineage. Eight of the yeast's own heme-pathway genes boosted, plus 16 copies of the soybean gene. Whole genome sequenced; stable for 150–200 generations without selection; no antibiotic-resistance genes.

Product

Cells are broken by bead mill, then removed by centrifugation and microfiltration; ultrafiltration concentrates the protein. Viable strain: not detected. Residual yeast DNA: about 0.2 mg/L, measured and stated.

How to write it

WeakOur system is biosafe because the engineered cells cannot escape.

"Cannot" is a promise. A reviewer will ask what was tested, how often, and what the detection limit was.

StrongThe production strain was not detected in any of five product lots by plating. The strain carries no antibiotic-resistance genes, and residual host DNA in the product is about 0.2 mg/L.

A test, a result, a count, and the leftovers stated as a number.

What ReLeaf takes from this

Our product, ACC deaminase, is secreted through a 0.2 µm membrane, so we never break cells open. That helps with host proteins. It does not prove containment: small vesicles and debris pass a 0.2 µm pore, and hollow fibres can develop pinholes.
